KATE GREENAWAY
1846–1901
==============================================================================

Born:  March 17, 1846, London
Died:  November 6, 1901, Frognal
Known as: painter, writer, illustrator, children's writer

ABOUT THEIR LIFE
----------------
• Catherine Greenaway (17 March 1846 – 6 November 1901) was an English
  Victorian artist and writer, known for her  children's book illustrations.
• She received her education in graphic design and art between 1858 and 1871
  from the Finsbury School of Art, the South Kensington School of Art, the
  Heatherley School of Art, and the Slade School of Fine Art.
• She began her career designing for the burgeoning greetings card market,
  producing Christmas and Valentine's cards.
• In 1879 wood-block engraver and printer Edmund Evans printed Under the
  Window, an instant best-seller, which established her reputation.
• Kate Greenaway was born in Hoxton, London, the second of four children, to a
  working-class family.
• Her mother, Elizabeth, was a dress maker and her father, John, an engraver
  who gave up steady employment with Ebenezer Landells' engraving firm to
  strike out on his own.

PROMINENT WORKS
---------------
1. Under the Window
2. Mother Goose or The Old Nursery Rhymes
3. Marigold Garden

WHY THEY MATTER
---------------
The depictions of children in imaginary 18th-century costumes in a Queen Anne
style were extremely popular in England and internationally, sparking the Kate
Greenaway style. The Kate Greenaway Medal, established in her honour in 1955,
was awarded annually by the Chartered Institute of Library and Information
Professionals in the UK to an illustrator of children's books. In 2022,
however, her name was controversially dropped as part of the terms of a
sponsorship deal with Yoto to rebrand the Carnegie Medal for Writing and the
Kate Greenaway Medal for Illustration under the single name “The Carnegie
Medals”.
