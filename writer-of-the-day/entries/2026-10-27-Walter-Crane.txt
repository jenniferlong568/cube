WALTER CRANE
1845–1915
==============================================================================

Born:  August 15, 1845, Liverpool
Died:  March 14, 1915, Horsham
Known as: painter, illustrator, draftsperson, visual artist

ABOUT THEIR LIFE
----------------
• Walter Crane (15 August 1845 – 14 March 1915) was an English artist, book
  illustrator, and prominent advocate for anti-authoritarian democratic
  socialism among British socialists and in the labour movement of the 19th
  century.
• An artistic activist—retroactively classifiable as an "artivist"—Crane
  produced a large body of both apolitical illustrations for primarily
  children's literature, though also contemporary (Victorian era) English
  adult-audience trade-books and general-interest literature, as well as
  visual works with explicit socio-political messaging.
• Crane's work featured some of the more colourful and detailed beginnings of
  the child-in-the-garden motifs that would characterize many nursery rhymes
  and children's stories for decades to come.
• He was part of the Arts and Crafts movement and produced an array of
  paintings, illustrations, children's books, ceramic tiles, wallpapers and
  other decorative arts.
• Crane was the second son of Thomas Crane, a portrait painter and
  miniaturist, and Marie Crane (née Kearsley), the daughter of a prosperous
  malt-maker.
• His elder brother Thomas would also go into illustration, and sister Lucy
  was a noted writer.

WHY THEY MATTER
---------------
He is considered to be the most influential, and among the most prolific,
children's book designers and illustrators of his generation and, along with
Randolph Caldecott and Kate Greenaway, one of the strongest contributors to
the child's nursery motif that the genre of English children's illustrated
literature would exhibit in its developmental stages in the late 19th century.
A member of Britain's first organized Marxist group, the Social Democratic
Federation (SDF), Crane's socio-political views evolved over time, and he
variously promoted social liberalism, radical liberalism, liberal socialism,
"evolutionary socialism", Fabianism, and democratic socialism. He was a
student who admired the masters of the Italian Renaissance, however he was
more influenced by the Elgin Marbles in the British Museum. A further and
important element in the development of his talent was the study of Japanese
colour-prints, the methods of which he imitated in a series of toy books,
which started a new fashion.
